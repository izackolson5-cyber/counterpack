How to respond to a chargeback: the evidence that matters

When a customer disputes a charge, you respond through your payment provider - Stripe, PayPal, Shopify Payments or whoever processed the payment - and you respond by the deadline shown in your dashboard. Miss the deadline and the dispute is usually lost automatically, whatever the facts were.

Start with the reason code

Every card dispute carries a reason code set by the card network, and the evidence that counts depends on it. The reason tells you what the customer's bank is actually asking. Stripe publishes the full list of dispute categories and the defence requirements per reason code; Shopify's chargeback reasons page maps the same ground for Shopify Payments. Read the one that matches your dispute before you collect anything.

What matters, reason by reason

Fraudulent ("I didn't make this purchase")

The bank wants proof the real cardholder took part: AVS and CVC check results from your processor, an IP address matching the billing or delivery area, tracking showing delivery to the verified address, any emails or messages from the customer about the order, and a history of previous undisputed orders from the same customer if you have one.

Product not received

Delivery proof is the whole game: the tracking number, a "delivered" scan dated before the dispute was opened, a signature if you have one, and the delivery address matching the order. If it genuinely has not arrived, a shipping status plus a refund often costs less than fighting.

Product unacceptable / not as described

Show the listing as it was at the time of sale: the description and photos the customer saw, your published returns policy and how it was shown at checkout, and any messages where the customer described the problem (or never raised one).

Credit not processed ("I returned it / cancelled and got no refund")

Your refund policy as displayed at purchase, the timeline of the return or cancellation, and why a refund was or was not due under that policy. If you actually owe the refund, issue it - disputing it costs a fee on top.

Duplicate

Evidence that the two charges were for two distinct things: separate order records, separate deliveries or downloads, separate receipts.

Subscription cancelled

Your cancellation terms, when the customer cancelled relative to the billing date, and records showing they kept using the service after the date they claim they cancelled.

Unrecognized ("I don't recognise this charge")

The receipt, what your statement descriptor means, and anything connecting the customer to the order - account email, delivery address, messages. These disputes often come from a confusing statement name rather than real fraud.

Rules that apply to every response

Answer the actual reason code. Strong evidence for the wrong reason does not count.
Only submit your own genuine records - real order data, real tracking, real messages. Never invent, guess or "reconstruct" evidence. That turns a lost dispute into a much bigger problem with your processor.
Keep it organised and short: a plain timeline, labelled documents, no repetition. A person at the bank reads this.
Submit before the deadline in your dashboard and keep copies of everything you sent.
The cardholder's bank decides the dispute. Good evidence improves your odds; nothing guarantees a win, and anyone who promises one is overselling.
