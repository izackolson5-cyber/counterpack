Stripe dispute reason codes: the 8 categories and what each one asks for

Each card network defines its own dispute reason codes - hundreds of them. Stripe organises every code into eight categories based on the type of claim and the evidence that answers it. Your dispute's category is the starting point for everything you submit: strong evidence aimed at the wrong category does not count. The official reference is Stripe's dispute reason code categories.

The eight categories

Fraudulent

The cardholder says they did not make or authorise the payment. The core evidence is anything linking the genuine cardholder to the order: AVS/CVC check results, the IP address and device data at purchase, delivery to the cardholder's address, account history, prior undisputed orders.

Unrecognized

The cardholder does not recognise the charge on their statement. Often a confusing statement descriptor rather than real fraud: show the receipt, what your descriptor means, and the details connecting the customer to the order.

Product not received

The customer says the goods or services never arrived. Delivery tracking with the matching address, download or login records for digital goods, and any messages where the customer acknowledges receipt.

Product unacceptable

Not as described, defective, counterfeit or misrepresented. Your accurate product description as shown at purchase, photos, and records of what you did to resolve the complaint before the dispute.

Credit not processed

The customer says they returned goods or cancelled and never got the promised refund. Your refund policy as displayed at purchase, the return or cancellation timeline, and why a refund was or was not due. If you actually owe the refund, issue it - fighting it costs a dispute fee on top.

Duplicate

The customer says they were charged twice for the same thing. Evidence that the charges were for distinct items: separate order records, deliveries, downloads or receipts.

Subscription canceled

The customer says they cancelled but were still charged. Your cancellation terms, when they cancelled relative to the billing date, and records showing continued use after the claimed cancellation.

General

A catch-all for processing and authorisation problems - declined authorisation, late presentment, incorrect amounts or account numbers. These are about the transaction mechanics rather than the customer relationship; your payment provider's dashboard usually shows exactly what the network is asking.

Three rules before you submit anything

Find your dispute's exact category and reason code in the Stripe dashboard first, then collect evidence for that claim - not for the dispute you expected.
Submit only genuine records, and never fabricate or alter documents - that turns a lost dispute into a much bigger problem with your processor. Organising what you already have, like a clear timeline or a summary built from your real order records, is legitimate. Inventing what you do not have is not.
The cardholder's bank decides. Good evidence improves your odds; nothing guarantees a win.
